Experiment 6 - priority aging versus starvation
===============================================

10 workers: 10 x uniform (16 cpu, 32GiB mem)
workload: 20000 jobs, 70% at priority 100 and 30% at priority 0, arriving at 40/sec
the cluster is deliberately undersized, so low-priority work only runs if the
ordering lets it

ORDERING                           CLASS   MEAN WAIT  P50 WAIT  P99 WAIT  MAX WAIT  CPU UTIL
fifo (priority ignored)            urgent  6.4m       6.4m      12.6m     12.7m     98.8%
fifo (priority ignored)            batch   6.4m       6.4m      12.6m     12.7m     98.8%
strict priority                    urgent  3.2m       3.2m      6.3m      6.4m      98.6%

priority + aging (10/s, no cap)    urgent  6.3m       6.3m      12.5m     12.6m     98.6%
priority + aging (10/s, no cap)    batch   6.7m       6.8m      12.8m     12.8m     98.6%

The aggregate wait hides the whole story here, which is why the table is split
by class. Read the gap between the urgent and batch rows: strict priority buys
urgent work a shorter wait and charges batch work for it, and aging is the
knob that decides how large that bill is allowed to get.
