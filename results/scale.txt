Three things to read out of this table.

Least-loaded and best-fit are strictly linear in fleet size: they score every
worker before choosing, so their cost tracks worker count and nothing else.

Round-robin's cost depends entirely on how soon it finds a worker that fits.
When one does, it is flat regardless of fleet size. When none does, it scans
everything and becomes the most expensive policy in the table.

The 'fits nowhere' rows are cheaper per worker for the scoring policies than
the rows where placement succeeds, because a worker that fails the fit check
is never scored. The expensive case for a scoring policy is a successful
placement, not a failed one.
